**Let me be honest with you.**

I didn't wake up one day and say ***"I want to be a Public Relations and Digital Communication professional."*** It wasn't that clean. It wasn't that certain. In fact, for a long time, I struggled to even communicate truly communicate with the people around me.

But sometimes, your greatest weakness points you toward your greatest purpose.

**I struggled before I found my footing.**

There was a time when storytelling felt impossible to me. Finding the right words, structuring my thoughts, expressing myself clearly these things didn't come naturally. I also had a quiet secret I carried with me. I show signs of being dyslexic. I haven't been formally diagnosed yet, but the signs have always been there the way I process information, the way I read, the extra effort it takes sometimes just to get through written content.

I never talked about it. I just pushed through.

And pushing through taught me something I couldn't have learned any other way *resilience*. It taught me that communication is not about being perfect. It's about being persistent. It's about showing up, practising , and refusing to let your struggles define your ceiling.

**So how did I end up in PR & Social Media?**

Honestly? I followed what felt right. I was always drawn to how messages are crafted, how brands tell their stories online, how a single well-placed caption or post can shift someone's perception overnight. Social media fascinated me not just as a user, but as a tool. A powerful, misunderstood, underutilised tool that, in the right hands, can build movements, change narratives, and connect communities across the world.

That fascination led me to enroll in Public Relations with Digital Communication at Accra Technical University and from there, I stopped waiting for opportunities and started creating them.

I volunteered. I showed up as an ambassador. I interned. I said yes to things that scared me. Each experience from representing brands at the National Youth Mentorship Summit and Ghana Tertiary Excellence Awards, to working as a PR and Production Intern at ATU TV Studio, to managing social media content and publicity for events, to stepping onto film sets with Next Pro Films added something to who I am.

I was building myself, one experience at a time.

**Then I found my why.**

Today I serve as a Communication & Marketing Assistant at the **Africa Dyslexia Organization**  and this role means more to me than any title ever could.

Part of my work involves managing their digital presence creating content, crafting social media messaging, and finding ways to use platforms like Instagram, Facebook, and LinkedIn to raise awareness about dyslexia and inclusive education in Africa. Every post, every caption, every campaign is an opportunity to reach a parent who is confused, a teacher who doesn't understand, or a child who just needs to know they are not alone.

Because I know what it feels like to sit in a room and wonder if your brain works differently from everyone else's. I know what it feels like to work twice as hard just to keep up. And I know how much it means when someone sees you really sees you and says ***"you are not broken, you just learn differently."***

That is the message ADO carries. And I get to help share it with the world one post at a time.

**What I've learned on this journey:**

* Your story even the painful parts is your greatest asset. Don't hide it.
* Social media is not just about likes and followers. It's about *connection and impact.*
* Communication is a skill. It can be learned, practised, and mastered. I am proof.
* Showing up consistently matters more than showing up perfectly.
* The work you do for others quietly shapes who you become.
* Vulnerability builds more connections than a perfect image ever will.

**Where I'm going.**

My goal is not just to build a career. It is to build a **personal brand** that stands for something authentic storytelling, purposeful communication, and meaningful digital impact. I want to grow into a PR and Social Media professional who doesn't just manage platforms but builds *communities* around causes that matter.

And beyond that, I want to **mentor the next generation of communicators** especially young people in Ghana who, like me, didn't start with confidence but have something powerful to say.

If you are a student, a young professional, or someone finding your voice know this: *you don't have to have it all figured out to start. You just have to start.*

I'm **Godfred Nana Yaw Ansah** PR & Digital Communication specialist, Social Media Manager, storyteller, and advocate for inclusive communication.

**Let's connect. Let's grow. Let's tell stories that matter.**

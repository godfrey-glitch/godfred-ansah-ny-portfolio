There's a misconception I keep hearing that Public Relations is becoming less relevant in the age of social media, algorithms, and content marketing. That anyone with a smartphone and a decent following can manage their own narrative now.

I couldn't disagree more.

If anything, the digital age hasn't made PR less important. It's made it more critical, more complex, and more unforgiving than it has ever been.

Here's why.

**The speed of information has changed everything**

Twenty years ago, a brand had time to respond to a crisis. A press release could be drafted, reviewed, approved, and distributed within a day or two. Today, a single tweet, a leaked screenshot, or a viral video can destroy a reputation in hours, sometimes minutes. The court of public opinion never closes, and it doesn't wait for your spokesperson to issue a statement.

In this environment, organisations that don't have a clear PR strategy aren't just unprepared, they're vulnerable. The digital age didn't remove the need for skilled communicators. It raised the stakes for what happens when you don't have one.

**Everyone has a platform, but not everyone has a strategy**

Yes, social media has given every individual and organisation a direct line to their audience. But having a platform and knowing how to use it strategically are two very different things. Posting frequently is not the same as communicating effectively. Going viral is not the same as building trust.

PR in the digital age is about more than pushing out content. It's about shaping perception, managing relationships, navigating crises, and telling a story that is consistent, credible, and compelling across every platform, every channel, and every touchpoint. That requires skill, intentionality, and a deep understanding of both communication and human behaviour.

**Trust is harder to earn and easier to lose**

We live in an era of misinformation, scepticism, and shrinking attention spans. Audiences are more discerning than ever; they can spot inauthenticity quickly, and they don't forget. One poorly worded statement, one tone-deaf campaign, one moment of silence during a crisis when your audience needed to hear from you any of these can erode trust that took years to build.

This is exactly where PR earns its value. Not just in the good times, when the brand story is easy to tell, but in the hard moments, when clarity, empathy, and honesty are the only tools that work.

**The organisations that understand this are winning**

Look at the organisations, globally and here in Ghana, that consistently maintain strong reputations, loyal communities, and meaningful public presence. Behind almost every one of them is a communications strategy that is intentional, responsive, and human. They understand that in the digital age, your reputation isn't just what you say about yourself it's what people say about you when you're not in the room.

And shaping that conversation is exactly what PR is for.

I'm studying Public Relations with Digital Communication at Accra Technical University, and every day I spend in the classroom and in the field at the Africa Dyslexia Organisation confirms what I believe deeply: the world doesn't need fewer communicators. It needs better ones.

The digital age didn't kill PR. It made it indispensable.

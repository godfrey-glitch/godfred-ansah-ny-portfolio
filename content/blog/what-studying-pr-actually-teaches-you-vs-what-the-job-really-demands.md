Let me be honest with you.

When I started studying Public Relations with Digital Communication at Accra Technical University, I had a certain picture in my head of what the field looked like. Press conferences. Media relations. Carefully crafted statements. A world of polished professionals managing big brand narratives from sleek offices.

The classroom taught me the theory behind all of that. The job taught me everything else.

**What the classroom teaches you**

Studying PR gives you a foundation that matters more than people give it credit for. You learn the history and principles of communication, why it works, how it works, and what happens when it doesn't. You study media relations, crisis communication, stakeholder management, writing for different audiences, and the ethics of public communication.

You learn frameworks. You learn how to think strategically about a message before you send it. You learn that communication is never just about words; it's about timing, audience, channel, and intent.

That grounding is real, and it is valuable. Don't let anyone tell you a communications degree teaches you nothing. It teaches you how to think like a communicator.

**What the job actually demands**

Then you step into the real world, and the real world moves faster, messier, and less predictably than any textbook scenario.

The job demands adaptability. Things change without warning. A campaign you spent a week planning might need to be scrapped in an hour because something shifted. A message that looked perfect on paper might land completely differently with a real audience.

The job demands speed. In a digital environment, slow communication is almost as damaging as wrong communication. You have to be able to think clearly, write well, and act decisively, often all at the same time.

The job demands emotional intelligence. So much of communications work is about people understanding what they need to hear, how they're feeling, and how to reach them where they are. No module prepares you for the specific human complexity you encounter in real organisations, with real stakeholders, dealing with real issues.

And the job demands humility. You will get things wrong. A post will underperform. A message will be misread. A campaign will miss its mark. The ability to take feedback, learn fast, and keep going is not taught in lectures; it's built through experience.

**The gap and why it's actually a gift**

I started my internship at the Africa Dyslexia Organisation as a complete beginner in practice. I knew the theory. I had the frameworks. But with social media management, newsletter campaigns, stakeholder communications, and event coordination, I was learning all of it in real time.

That gap between what I studied and what the job demanded wasn't a failure of my education. It was an invitation to grow. The classroom gave me the map. The internship put me on the road.

And here's what I've come to believe: the best communications professionals are the ones who never stop being students. Not just of theory but of people, of culture, of the ever-shifting digital landscape they work in every day.

If you're a PR or communications student in Ghana, feeling like the gap between your degree and the real world is too wide, I want you to know that gap is normal. It's not a sign that you're behind. It's a sign that you're exactly where you're supposed to be.

Close the gap one experience at a time. That's all any of us are doing.

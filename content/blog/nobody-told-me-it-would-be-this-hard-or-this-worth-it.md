There's a version of "student and intern at the same time" that looks great on a CV. Then there's the lived version, the 6 am alarms, the assignments due the same week as work deadlines, the evenings where you're not sure if you should be studying or finishing that content calendar.

I've been living the second version. And I wouldn't trade it.

I'm currently a Public Relations with Digital Communication student at Accra Technical University in Accra. At the same time, I work as a Communications and Marketing Assistant at the Africa Dyslexia Organization, I currently volunteered as an Event Coordinator for the Africa Real Estate Festival 2026, and am building my own EdTech platform, Nyansa AI, on the side.

Written out like that, it sounds impressive. In the middle of it, it mostly just sounds like a lot.

There were weeks where I felt like I was failing at everything simultaneously, not studying enough, not contributing enough at work, not building fast enough. The overlap between deadlines at university and responsibilities at ADO wasn't always manageable. Some days, I sat in a lecture hall thinking about a newsletter I hadn't finished. Other days, I sat at my desk at work thinking about an assignment I hadn't started.

Nobody tells you about that part.

But here's what nobody tells you about the other side of it either: when you're doing real work in your field while you're still studying it, something clicks in a way that a classroom alone can't replicate. I wasn't just learning about communications in theory; I was writing newsletters, managing social media, producing content, and coordinating events in real time. Every lecture started to mean something more concrete. Every assignment felt more grounded.

My internship at ADO taught me things no module could. It taught me how to communicate with purpose, how the right message at the right time can shift how people think about something as misunderstood as dyslexia. It taught me how to stay calm when things don't go to plan, how to collaborate, how to take feedback without taking it personally, and how to find your voice in a professional environment when you're still figuring out who you are.

It also pushed me to build Nyansa AI because being at the intersection of education, communication, and technology every single day made me hungry to create something of my own.

I'm not going to pretend it's been easy. There are still hard weeks. There are still moments where the juggle feels impossible. But I've learned that discomfort and growth almost always arrive together. The pressure of doing both school and real work has shaped me in ways that comfort never could.

If you're a student in Ghana balancing studies with work, internships, or your own projects I want you to know that the chaos you're living through is building something in you. It doesn't always feel like it. But it is.

Keep showing up. Even on the hard days. Especially on the hard days.

Your future self will thank you for it.
